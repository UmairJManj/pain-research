Spinal pain · EEG · Machine learning

Is a painful spinal segment processed differently by the brain?

I study chronic low back pain by vibrating the painful segment and a healthy segment of the same person and comparing the cortical EEG response between them. The aim is a biomarker that rests on a mechanism.

The planned protocol in two minutes: participant setup, tactor placement over the paraspinal muscles, and the expected EEG contrast. The final panel shows the hypothesis, not measured data.

The question

Earlier EEG studies found larger cortical responses to stimulation of the low back in people with chronic low back pain than in people without it (Clauwaert et al., 2024; Jacobs et al., 2016). Dörig et al. (2026) found no group difference.

All of these compare groups of people. I compare segments within one person: the segment that hurts against one that does not. If the painful segment is amplified, that difference should show at the sensory cortex, with each participant as their own control.

L3

Painful segment, the level stimulated in the closest EEG precedent (Clauwaert et al., 2019, 2024).

T7

Healthy segment, chosen well clear of the low back.

Protocol

  1. The participant lies prone on a plinth with an EEG cap in place.
  2. The painful segment and a healthy segment are marked on the skin.
  3. Tactor pairs sit 3 cm either side of the midline at each marked level, with a further pair on the calf.
  4. The painful pair and the healthy pair vibrate in turn.
  5. The cortical response to each is recorded over the sensory cortex and compared within the person.

Why within person

Group comparisons mix pain with everything else that differs between people. Comparing two segments of one back removes most of that, and none of the studies above test this contrast directly.

Pilot 1: what the data said

One participant, 75 tactor placements, 73 kept. I report the result as it came out, including the part I had to withdraw.

92 to 99%

Withdrawn

My first pass decoded vibration on versus off at this accuracy. The hold window was 8.5 s against 3 s comparison windows, and the shorter windows biased the log power estimate. Duration matched, the effect was zero. The decoder was reading window length.

1.39

No steady state response

Highest signal to noise ratio over the posterior central electrodes, against a feasibility criterion of 2.0. The vibrator left no trace in the EEG at the frequency used.

3

Fixes for the next recording

Measure the drive frequency before the session with an accelerometer, choose a frequency the scalp can follow, and write the trigger into the EEG file.

Until those three hold, the level question cannot be asked. The per level figures are kept so the null result can be checked by eye.

About

I am a doctoral researcher in Health and Artificial Intelligence at Auckland University of Technology, building an AI based biomarker for chronic low back pain. I also collect and analyse study data at the Centre for Chiropractic Research, New Zealand College of Chiropractic.

My training is in engineering: a BE in Computer Engineering and an MS in Computational Science and Engineering from NUST, Islamabad. Most of my papers deal with one problem: accuracy that looks high because of how the data were split or confounded. I check the evaluation before I report a number.

Methods

EEG and IMU signal analysis · vibrotactile stimulation · spectral and time frequency analysis · Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn · subject level evaluation, leakage and confounding checks
